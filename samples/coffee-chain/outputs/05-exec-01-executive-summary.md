## The problem
- **Stores run out of core items 4–5 times a week.** 54 stockouts were reported in 12 weeks, and the real number is likely higher. Oat milk and large cups account for 35 of them. "Customers walk out."[^1]
- **We write off ≈ $182k of stock a year.** Pastries are about half of that (≈ $96k). Emergency couriers cost another ≈ $19k.[^2]
- **Ordering takes 36–50 staff hours a week**, about $70k–$97k a year of staff time. Most of it is managers counting by hand and the coordinator retyping orders.[^3]
- **Ordering depends on one person.** When the coordinator was off sick in August, two stores got nothing for a week. At 17 stores her retyping alone grows to 8.5–11 hours a week.[^4]

## Why it happens
- **No one owns the stock rules.** Target stock levels haven't been updated since 2024 while the chain kept growing, so managers order by guesswork.
- **The data exists but doesn't reach the decision.** The tills record exactly what sold, and the warehouse system knows what is in stock. Neither is used when ordering.
- **There is no standard way to order.** Each store uses its own spreadsheet, so orders are retyped, errors creep in (2–3 a week), and nobody is accountable when stock goes missing.[^5]

## What we recommend (quick wins first)
1. **Name owners** for ordering end to end, for the stock rules, and for stock once it reaches a store. *Result: clear accountability. This is a decision, not a cost.*
2. **Start measuring now.** Set up one shared log for stockouts, emergency orders and wrong deliveries, and get all 12 stores completing waste logs. *Result: a trusted baseline within 4–6 weeks.*
3. **Remove the one-person risk.** Write down the coordinator's routines, train a backup, and give everyone their own system login. *Result: no repeat of August.*
4. **Set clear rules for substitutions and deliveries, and match pastry orders to sales** (if pastries are in scope). *Result: fewer wrong-item stockouts, and pastry waste down 15–20% (≈ $14k–$19k a year, estimate).*
5. **Introduce one order form for all stores, loaded straight into the warehouse system, and reset stock levels from sales** (Feb–May 2027). Then **use till sales to suggest order quantities** before the July openings. *Result: retyping falls from 6–8 hours to under 1 hour a week, stockouts are halved, and waste falls 10–20%.*[^6]

## What it takes
- **Cost: TBD.** Steps 1–4 are mostly staff time. Step 5 needs IT set-up quotes, due 31 Jan. Everything recommended for this year must fit within the ~$60k budget. The warehouse system's future is a separate decision. At the estimated benefits, the first wave pays back within 18 months if it costs **under ≈ $27k–$42k**.[^7]
- **Timeline:**
  - Now to mid-Feb: owners, rules and logging.
  - Feb–May: the order form and stock levels.
  - By July: sales-based ordering.
  - No new system is needed this year.
- **People:** the Operations Director (sponsor), the Head of Finance, the warehouse coordinator and a backup, store managers, and IT (Josh plus the contractor).

## If we do nothing
- Waste, courier costs and stockouts continue.
- Ordering effort grows by about 40% as we move to 17 stores. That likely means a second coordinator, which goes against the growth goal.
- The August failure could repeat across 17 stores during the summer peak.
- The warehouse system loses vendor support in **Dec 2027**, and there is no plan for it yet.
