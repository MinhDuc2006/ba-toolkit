### Slide 1: We ask for three owners and a go-ahead by 30 November, so that ordering works before the five new stores open
- Name owners for ordering end to end, for the stock rules, and for stock at the store.
- Allow logging and rule changes during the Dec–Jan freeze, and decide whether pastries are in scope.
- Agree in principle to fund the Feb–Jun work from the ~$60k budget. Quotes by 31 Jan.

**Speaker notes:** Start with the ask so the room knows what we need from them today. Everything this month is a decision rather than spending. The money decision comes in January, when there are real quotes. Emphasise **30 November**: after that we lose the freeze window for building a baseline.

### Slide 2: Stores run out of core items 4–5 times a week, while we write off ≈ $182k of stock a year
**Visual:** a bar chart of reported stockouts by item over 12 weeks:
- oat milk 21
- 16oz cups 14
- 16oz lids 9
- vanilla syrup 6
- decaf beans 4

Next to it, a callout box: *Waste $182k/yr (pastries $96k) · Couriers $19k/yr*.

**Speaker notes:** These are self-reported stockouts, so the real number is higher. Oat milk and large cups alone are 35 of the 54. Waste is Finance's recorded figure. Priya's ~$250k estimate is plausible, but we'll prove it with complete logs rather than argue it today. Emphasise **4–5 a week**.

### Slide 3: Ordering depends on one person and won't survive 17 stores as it is
- Ordering takes 36–50 staff hours a week (≈ $70k–$97k a year).
- The coordinator retypes ~480 order lines every Monday (6–8 h), with about 2–3 errors a week.
- In August she was off sick for a week, and two stores got nothing.
- At 17 stores, retyping alone grows to 8.5–11 h a week.

**Speaker notes:** This is the growth risk. Without change, the next step is a second coordinator, which goes directly against the goal of scaling with one. Emphasise **"two stores got nothing for a week"**.

### Slide 4: The causes are missing owners, out-of-date stock rules and unused data, not people working badly
- Target stock levels haven't been updated since 2024, and no one owns them.
- The tills know what sold and the warehouse knows what's in stock, but neither is used when ordering.
- Twelve different store spreadsheets mean retyping, errors and no accountability.

**Speaker notes:** Every root cause is something we control: a rule, an owner, a format or a data flow. This is about the system, not individuals. Mei and the managers are compensating for missing rules. Emphasise **"no one owns the stock rules"**.

### Slide 5: Most fixes are rules, owners and one standard order form. No new system is needed this year
**Visual:** a small two-column table, *Now (low cost)* vs *Feb–Jul (investment)*.
- **Now (low cost):**
  - owners
  - shared log
  - backup and logins
  - substitution and delivery rules
  - pastry review
- **Feb–Jul (investment):**
  - one order form loaded directly
  - stock levels reset from sales
  - sales-suggested orders

**Speaker notes:** Quick wins come first because they fix causes cheaply and build trust. The bigger step, sales-based ordering, waits for a March go/no-go check. Emphasise **"no new system this year"**. That answers the "another system nobody uses properly" concern.

### Slide 6: The first wave pays back within 18 months if it costs under ≈ $27k–$42k. Quotes are due 31 January
**Visual:** the comparison table from the business case, with three columns (Do nothing / Quick wins / Full, staged). Show these rows:
- annual cash benefit: $0 / $19k–$29k / $28k–$46k
- payback ceiling: — / $27k–$42k / $40k–$68k
- key risk

**Speaker notes:** All benefits are estimates. Costs are TBD until the vendor and IT quotes are in, and I won't put a number on them before then. Staff time saved is capacity, not cash, unless hours are cut. The biggest avoided cost, a second coordinator, still needs Finance to cost it. Emphasise the **payback ceiling** as the test the quotes must pass.

### Slide 7: A staged plan delivers before the July openings and works around the December–January freeze
**Visual:** a Now / Next / Later timeline:
- Now: mid-Nov to mid-Feb
- Next: mid-Feb to mid-May
- Later: mid-May onwards

Show checkpoints at 30 Nov (decisions), 31 Jan (quotes and vendor answers), March (go/no-go) and July (new stores).

**Speaker notes:**
- The freeze is used to build the baseline. Logging is low-risk.
- The new stores will start on the new process rather than being migrated later.
- The warehouse system's end of support in Dec 2027 is a separate decision for around September.

Emphasise **"stores 13–17 start on the new process from day one"**.

### Slide 8 (appendix): Sources and assumptions
