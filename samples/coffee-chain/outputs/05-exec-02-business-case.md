# Business case: Stock ordering improvements

**Harbour Lane Coffee Co.** · **Date:** 12 Nov 2026 · **Prepared by:** Business Analyst, stock ordering review
**For decision:** choose an option, and approve the first wave within the ~$60k one-off budget once quotes arrive (31 Jan 2027).

*Status: draft. **Costs are TBD** until quotes are in. Benefits are **estimates** built from 12 weeks of operating data and Finance's staff rates, and Finance has not yet reviewed them. Every figure is listed with its basis in §8.*

---

## 1. Background and objective
- Ordering runs on paper counts, 12 different store spreadsheets, email, WhatsApp and a 9-year-old warehouse system. One coordinator retypes every order.
- We plan to grow from 12 to 17 stores by July 2027. The sponsor's view is that "the way we order stock today won't survive 17 stores".
- **Objective:** before the new stores open,
  - halve core-item stockouts,
  - cut recorded waste by 10–20%,
  - scale to 17 stores with one coordinator,
  - give managers time back on the floor.

## 2. Options
- **Option 0, Do nothing.** Keep ordering as it is today.
- **Option 1, Quick wins plus a standard order form.**
  - Name owners.
  - Start a shared log and complete waste logs.
  - Train a backup coordinator and set up individual logins.
  - Agree substitution and delivery rules.
  - Review pastry orders.
  - Introduce one order form, loaded straight into the warehouse system.
  - Reset stock levels from sales.
  - Start a monthly scorecard.
- **Option 2, Full recommendation, staged *(recommended)*.** Everything in Option 1, plus **till sales used to suggest order quantities** and **warehouse stock shown when ordering**. This extra step is released only after a go/no-go check in March 2027.
- **In every option:**
  - Ask the warehouse system vendor now for its upgrade path.
  - Treat the *upgrade or replace* decision (by ~Sep 2027) as a **separate business case**, likely funded next financial year.[^1]

## 4. Benefits
**Cash benefits (estimates, a year once live):**
- **Waste:**
  - Option 1 counts **pastries only**: 15–20% × $96k = **$14k–$19k**, and only if pastries are in scope.
  - Option 2 counts the **sponsor's full target**: 10–20% × $182k = **$18k–$36k**.
- **Emergency couriers:**
  - Option 1: 25–50% fewer × $19k = **$5k–$10k**.
  - Option 2: about 50% fewer (in line with halving stockouts) = **≈ $10k**.
- **Sales lost to stockouts: TBD.** This is not measured today. The new log will provide the first evidence.

| | Option 1 | Option 2 |
|---|---|---|
| **Total cash benefit a year** | **≈ $19k–$29k** | **≈ $28k–$46k** |

**Non-cash benefits.** These free up capacity. They only become cash if hours are actually reduced.
- **Coordinator time released:** (6–8 h − under 1 h) × $34 × 52 = **≈ $9k–$12k a year equivalent**, in both options.
- **No second coordinator needed at 17 stores:** **TBD**, for Finance to cost. *Illustration only, not counted:* a 38-hour week at $34/h ≈ $67k a year.
- **Manager time (mainly Option 2):** every 30 minutes a week saved per store = 0.5 × 12 × $38 × 52 ≈ **$12k a year** (≈ $17k at 17 stores). No target has been set yet.
- **Risk reduced:**
  - ordering continues when one person is away,
  - an audit trail of who changed what,
  - named accountability for stock,
  - a trusted baseline for the board.

## 6. Risks and mitigations
| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| The warehouse system's import can't take store orders | Confirm the format with the vendor by 31 Jan, before spending. Fallback: the standard form still removes most of the "read carefully" work |
| Low adoption ("another system nobody uses properly") | Simplify the tools people already use (a spreadsheet form and a shared log). Pilot in 2 stores. Track use at 12/12 stores |
| IT capacity: one generalist plus a contractor | Confirm Josh's Feb–Jun time now. Keep the sales-based step behind the March go/no-go |
| Reported stockouts **rise** at first once they are logged properly | Agree this with leadership before the log goes live, so it isn't read as things getting worse |
| Money spent on a warehouse system that loses support in Dec 2027 | Keep spend on it minimal. The order form works with any future system. Run the upgrade-or-replace decision separately |

## 7. Recommendation
**Option 2, staged.**
1. Commit to Option 1 now. Most of it is rules, owners and a standard form, which tackle the main causes cheaply.
2. Release the sales-based ordering step at a **March 2027 checkpoint**, and only if all three checks pass:
   - the import works in the 2-store pilot,
   - till items can be matched to stock items,
   - IT time is confirmed.

Option 1 alone delivers most of the cash benefit. Option 2 is what makes the stockout target and the manager-time goal hold up at 17 stores.
