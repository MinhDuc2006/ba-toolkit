# Interview — Store manager

**Date:** 2026-10-23 · **Interviewee:** Daniel Okafor, Store Manager, Harbour Lane Docklands (busiest store) · **Duration:** 45 min

## How ordering works (in Daniel's words)
- "Every Sunday after close I walk the store and back room with a clipboard and count everything on the stock sheet." Takes ~1–1.5 hours.
- Monday morning he types the counts into the store's Excel order file and works out what to order: "I look at what we've got and guess what we'll need. There's a par level column but nobody's updated those since 2024."
- Saves the file and emails it to the warehouse (orders@ inbox) — deadline 10am Monday. "If I'm late, I text Mei and hope."
- Deliveries come Wednesday or Thursday. Driver drops boxes; a shift lead checks them "if they're not slammed". Often not checked against the order.
- If something runs out mid-week: call or WhatsApp Mei for an emergency delivery (courier), or send a barista to another store to borrow stock. "Happens most weeks in summer."
- Dairy milk: he calls Valley Dairy directly, daily order by phone or their web form. Pastries: standing order with Crumb & Co., changed by email "maybe twice a year".

## Pain points
- Running out of oat milk and large cups is the worst — "customers walk out". Also sometimes vanilla syrup.
- Substitutions without warning: "We ordered 10 cases of 16oz cups and got 12oz — no one told us."
- Pastry waste: "We bin maybe 15–20 pastries a day on weekdays." Standing order hasn't been adjusted for the quieter winter trade.
- His Excel file is different from other stores' — "I added my own columns." New managers learn from whoever trains them.
- Can't see what the warehouse actually has, so he over-orders "just in case" on things that ran out before.
- Total time on ordering per week: "3 hours easy, plus chasing."

## Systems he uses
- TillPoint POS — sales by product, he can export reports but "never has time".
- Excel order file on the store laptop. Email. WhatsApp group with other managers and Mei.
- Valley Dairy's web form.

## Ideas he mentioned
- "If the till knows what we sold, why am I counting everything by hand?"
- Wants a confirmation of what's coming before the delivery.
